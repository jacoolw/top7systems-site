Getting Started

Stampr is a GPS field camera designed for professionals who need location-stamped photos and video with metadata. It burns GPS coordinates, altitude, bearing, street address, and optional voice notes directly onto your photos, and embeds the GPS data into the photo file itself (EXIF), so your shots pin correctly in Google Photos, GIS software, and claims tools.

The app has three tabs, plus a fourth if you turn on Field Notes:

  • Camera: Take GPS-stamped photos and videos with a live metadata overlay.
  • Notes (optional): Speak or type GPS-stamped notes without a photo, kept in an open log. See Field Notes.
  • Library: Browse, search, and organize your photos, videos, and notes by project and tags.
  • Settings: Customise the stamp format, toggle features, and manage your Pro purchase.
1

Grant Permissions

When you first open Stampr, allow access to your camera and location (choose "While using the app" with precise location). Microphone access is needed only if you use voice notes or field notes, or record video. You can change these anytime in Android Settings > Apps > Stampr > Permissions.

2

Take Your First Photo

Point the camera and tap the shutter button. Stampr saves the photo immediately and stamps it with your GPS data, address, and timestamp, all frozen at the exact moment you pressed the shutter.

3

Add a Voice Note (Optional)

After each photo, hold the microphone button to record a voice note. Stampr transcribes it on-device (a one-time ~32 MB speech model download is offered first), and on supported devices an on-device AI tidies the dictation into a clean caption with suggested tags. Prefer fully hands-free? Turn on Hands-Free mode in Settings and Stampr records automatically after every shot, stopping when you stop talking.

4

Review in Library

Switch to the Library tab to see all your stamped photos. Tap any photo for full details, metadata, and sharing options.

Using the Camera

The Camera tab is where you take GPS-stamped photos and videos. The live stamp overlay at the top shows exactly what will be burned onto your image.

Stamp Overlay

The live GPS data is displayed at the top of the camera view. This is exactly what will be stamped onto your photo. It updates in real time as you move. The overlay shows:

  • Street address (reverse-geocoded)
  • GPS coordinates in Decimal, DMS, UTM, or MGRS format
  • Altitude (metres or feet), bearing (true or magnetic north), and accuracy
  • Date and time

The accuracy readout is colour-coded: green within 5 m, amber within 15 m, red beyond that. If there's no GPS fix yet, the overlay shows "ACQUIRING GPS…", and photos taken without a fix are stamped "NO GPS FIX" rather than misleading zero coordinates.

With Map on Stamp enabled in Settings, a small OpenStreetMap inset showing your position is added to the stamp as well.

Need a clearer view of the scene? Tap the overlay to collapse it into a compact pill (still tinted with your GPS fix quality); tap again to expand it.

Photo / Video Mode

Switch between PHOTO and VIDEO with the mode picker above the shutter. See Stamped Video below for details.

Zoom & Lenses

Pinch to zoom or tap the preset buttons above the shutter. On phones with an ultra-wide camera you also get a 0.5× preset.

Level Indicator

A subtle bubble level in the centre of the viewfinder shows when your horizon is straight; it turns green when you're level, in both portrait and landscape. Great for straight-on documentation shots. You can turn it off in Settings.

Require GPS Fix

If your work demands coordinates on every shot, enable Require GPS Fix in Settings. The shutter will then wait for a GPS lock, with a "Capture Anyway" escape hatch if you need the shot regardless.

Focus, Front Camera & Volume Buttons

Tap the viewfinder to focus there. Long-press to lock focus and exposure (the viewfinder shows AE/AF LOCK), and tap again to unlock. The switch-camera button flips to the front camera. The volume buttons work as a shutter, which is easier with gloves on: press one to take a photo, or to start and stop a video.

Flash & Resolution

Tap the flash icon to cycle the flash off, auto, and on. In video mode the same button turns the torch on or off. Enable Maximum Resolution in Settings to capture at your phone's full sensor resolution (larger files).

Tip: Make sure Location is enabled in Android Settings (with precise location for Stampr) for the most accurate GPS data. The colour-coded accuracy indicator on the stamp overlay shows how precise your current fix is.

Voice Notes & AI Cleanup

Voice notes add context that would be tedious to type: site conditions, observations, measurements, or instructions. Stampr transcribes them on-device and can burn the text directly onto the photo.

The Speech Model

Transcription runs entirely on your phone using the open-source Whisper speech-recognition model: no cloud, no account, and it works on every Android phone, not just flagships. The first time you use voice notes, Stampr offers a one-time model download (about 32 MB); you can also start it from Settings → Voice Recognition Model. After that, transcription works completely offline.

Three Voice Modes

Choose how voice notes work in Settings → Voice Notes:

  • Prompt After Photo (default): After each shot, a sheet appears. Hold the microphone button to record, release to stop.
  • Hands-Free: Recording starts automatically after every shutter press and stops by itself about two seconds after you stop talking. From pocket to documented photo without touching the screen, ideal with gloves on.
  • Off: No voice prompt. You can still add notes later from the photo detail screen.

AI Note Cleanup

On devices with Android's on-device AI (Gemini Nano, on recent Pixel and Galaxy flagships), Stampr's AI Note Cleanup tidies your dictation into a clear, professional caption and suggests searchable tags, entirely on-device. "um so there's a crack on the uh north wall" becomes "Crack on north wall." The verbatim transcript is always kept too: you can see the original on any photo's detail screen.

On devices without on-device AI, the verbatim transcription is used as-is, so nothing is lost.

Important: your photo is saved the moment you press the shutter, before the voice note. If anything interrupts the recording, the photo is never lost.

Stamped Video

Record site walkthroughs with the GPS stamp burned onto the footage.

1

Switch to VIDEO

Tap VIDEO in the mode picker above the shutter. The shutter button turns red.

2

Record

Tap to start recording (with sound), tap again to stop. Recordings cap at 3 minutes. The GPS data, address, and time are captured at the moment recording starts.

3

Stamped & Kept in Your Library

Stampr burns the same stamp band your photos get (plus your watermark or logo) onto the video and keeps it in the Library with your photos, in the same projects, reports, and exports. Look for the "Video saved to Library" confirmation. What you say while filming is transcribed on the phone into the video's note. To also keep a copy in your gallery's Movies/Stampr folder, turn on Auto-Save to Gallery in Settings.

Note: each video uses one of the 15 free captures a month, the same as a photo. Videos aren't packed into project files; share them individually from the video's screen.

Field Notes

Some things worth recording have no picture: a radio check, the fork you took, a measurement, what a witness said. Field Notes lets you speak or type a note without taking a photo, and stamps each one with the time and your GPS position, just like a photo. Notes go into an open log that you keep for a whole job or search until you close it, together with the photos and videos you take along the way.

1

Turn It On

Field Notes is off by default. Go to Settings → Capture and turn on Field Notes. A Notes tab appears between Camera and Library.

2

Start a Log

On the Notes tab, tap Start Log. It's named with the date and time, like "Log 22 Oct, 08:12"; type over it with the job or search name. Until you close it, every note, photo, and video goes into this log.

3

Speak or Type

Tap the microphone and talk, then tap again to save. Each note lands in the log with its time and position, and turns into text a moment later.

4

Close and Share

When the job or search is over, open the ⋮ menu at the top right and choose Close Log, then Close and Share Report to send the whole log as a PDF report.

Ways to Record

  • Tap the microphone to start, and tap again to save.
  • Hold it while you talk, and let go to save.
  • Press a volume button to start, and again to save. Handy with gloves on.
  • Tap the keyboard button to type a note, then Save Note.
  • From outside the app, the Record note shortcut and the Stampr Note Quick Settings tile open the Notes tab already recording (see Quick Settings Tile & Shortcuts).

While a note records, the screen stays on and the phone buzzes briefly as it starts and stops. Tap the X beside the microphone to throw a recording away. A note can run for up to five minutes; at five minutes Stampr saves it, and you can tap the microphone to carry on in a new one.

Nothing is lost: a note is saved the moment it stops. If you press Back, switch tabs, or leave the app in the middle of a note, what you've said so far is kept as a note.

Time and Place

Each note is stamped with where you were when you started it: coordinates in your chosen format, altitude, and GPS accuracy, plus the street address when Street Address is on. There's no compass bearing on a note, as you weren't aiming the camera at anything. The strip above the microphone shows your current position; when its dot is green, that's the position your next note gets.

If there's no fresh fix when you start, Stampr uses the first one that arrives within a minute. If none arrives, the note is still kept and marked NO GPS FIX, the same as a photo. The strip says why there's no position (Waiting for GPS…, Phone location is off, or No location access) and offers a button to fix it.

From Speech to Text

Notes are turned into text on the phone by the same Whisper speech model as photo voice notes, one note at a time, so a quick run of notes simply queues up. Nothing is uploaded. If the speech model isn't on the phone yet, recordings are still kept: the Notes tab offers the one-time download (about 32 MB), and waiting notes are transcribed once it's in. If a transcription fails, the note keeps its recording and Stampr tries again; you can also open the note and tap Transcribe.

On phones with on-device AI, AI Note Cleanup tidies notes and suggests tags, as it does for photos. The original transcript is always kept.

The Open Log

A log is a project that stays open: while it's open, every new note, photo, and video goes into it, so a whole search reads as one timeline, oldest first, with a divider for each day. The Notes tab shows the open log, with its name and a count at the top and the newest entry at the bottom. The ⋮ menu manages it:

  • Start a New Log…: begin another log, which closes the current one.
  • Switch Log… (or Open a Log… when none is open): open an existing log. Any project can be one.
  • Close Log: new notes, photos, and videos stop going into it. It stays in the Library as a project, and you can open it again any time.
  • Export PDF Report and Share Log as Text: see Sharing Notes below.

The camera button beside the microphone takes you to the camera, and the photos and videos you take go into the open log. Notes made with no log open are kept too, under No Project, and can be filed later.

Good to know: the open log is your default project, the one new photos are filed into. Starting a log makes it the default, and closing it clears the default, so photos taken after the search stay out of its log.

Reading and Editing a Note

Tap the play button on a note to hear it right in the log, or tap the note to open it. On a note's screen you can play the recording, read its stamp, correct the text (the original transcript stays one tap away), add or remove tags, move it to another project or log, see where it was taken on a map with its coordinates, MGRS / USNG reference, altitude, and accuracy, share it, or delete it.

Notes also appear in the Library and in their project, alongside your photos and videos. A project with notes can be read as a log too: tap the list icon at the top of the project.

Sharing Notes

  • PDF report: ⋮ → Export PDF Report, or Close and Share Report as you close the log. Notes sit among the photos and videos in time order, marked VOICE NOTE or TYPED NOTE, with their words, tags, and location, and with pins on the map overview. A report of notes alone is titled "Stampr Field Notes".
  • As text: ⋮ → Share Log as Text sends the whole log, oldest first, as plain text you can paste into a message, an email, or an incident record. One note goes the same way from its screen with Share as Text, or with its recording attached using Share with Recording.
  • With photos: share a selection from the Library that includes notes, and the notes travel as the message text and as a Notes.txt file beside the photos and report.
  • Data export: CSV, KML, and GeoJSON exports include notes, so a search's notes can go straight into Google Earth or a GIS tool.
  • Project files: Share Project File includes notes and their recordings.
Free and unlimited: field notes never use one of your 15 free captures a month. Only photos and videos count.

Turning Field Notes off hides the Notes tab, the shortcut, and the tile. Notes you've already made stay in the Library.

Photo Library

Browsing Photos

The Library tab shows all your photos, videos, and field notes in a grid sorted by date. Use the filter menu to narrow by time period, project, or tag. Use the search bar to find items by notes (including the original transcript), tags, address, or project name; type "video" to list your videos, or "notes" to list your field notes.

Map View

Toggle the map icon to see your photos, videos, and notes plotted on an OpenStreetMap view. Tap any marker to open its details. Only items with valid GPS coordinates appear on the map.

Selecting Multiple Items

Tap "Select" to enter selection mode, then tap photos, videos, or notes to choose them. "Select All" picks up everything currently listed. The toolbar can then:

  • Move to project: file the whole selection into a project in one go, or create a new project on the spot. Every moved photo is re-stamped so the project name on the image matches where it now lives.
  • Share: send the selection, with the PDF report attached.
  • Delete: remove the selection after a confirmation.

The same toolbar is available inside a project, where "No Project" means "remove these from this project".

Photo Details

Tap any photo in the Library to see its full metadata. The detail view shows everything that was captured with the photo:

  • Stamped photo: The full image with GPS data burned on; tap for fullscreen
  • Project: Tap to change or assign a project
  • Notes & tags: AI-cleaned caption (editable), the original transcript, and tag chips you can add or remove
  • Before / After: Link this photo with another to document repairs; share the pair as a side-by-side composite
  • Location: Map, street address (editable), coordinates, and the UTM reference; tap the coordinates to open them in your maps app
  • GPS Data: Altitude, bearing, tilt/roll, accuracy, and capture time

Editable Address

Reverse-geocoded addresses aren't always perfect: rural sites, new subdivisions, and large lots often come back wrong. Tap the address on the detail screen to correct it, and Stampr re-stamps the photo with your corrected address. This is free for everyone.

Before / After Pairs

Tap Link Before/After Photo to pair two photos; the older one automatically becomes the "before". Once linked, the share menu gains Share Before/After, which exports a labelled side-by-side composite image. Perfect for documenting repairs.

Sharing One Photo

The share button offers Share Stamped Photo (with the PDF report), Share Original (the unstamped capture), and Share Before/After when the photo is paired.

Tip: You can edit notes, tags, the address, or the project at any time. The stamp is re-rendered from the original unstamped photo using the same options it was first stamped with, so quality and consistency are never lost.

Projects

Projects help you organize photos by job site, inspection, or any grouping that makes sense for your work.

Creating a Project

In the Library tab, tap "+ New" next to the Projects row. Give it a name, pick a colour, and optionally set it as your default project.

Assigning Photos

From the photo detail screen, tap the project card to move a photo to a different project or remove it from a project. If you have no project yet, or want a new one, choose "New Project…" from the same sheet and the photo is filed into it as soon as it is created.

To file a batch, use "Select" in the Library or inside a project, choose the photos, and tap the move icon in the toolbar. Either way the stamp on each photo is re-rendered so the project name burned onto the image matches the project it now belongs to.

Default Project

Set a default project so all new photos are automatically assigned to it. This is useful when you're on-site and want every photo tagged to the current job. With Field Notes on, the default project is also the open log on the Notes tab, so new notes go into it too.

Renaming & Deleting

Open the project menu to rename or delete. After renaming, Stampr asks if you want to update the project name on all existing photos. When deleting, you can keep the photos unassigned or delete them too.

Sharing, Reports & Data Export

PDF Field Reports

When you share photos, Stampr attaches a professional PDF report, the document you hand to a client or claims assessor. The report includes:

  • A branded cover header (your logo appears here with Pro)
  • A map overview page with numbered pins matching the photo list
  • Every photo embedded at quality, with its full metadata table, notes, and tags
  • Videos as a still frame marked VIDEO with the clip's length, and field notes as their words, tags, and location

Don't need the report? Turn off Include Report When Sharing in Settings to share just the photos.

You can also send the report on its own. Open a project, then choose Export PDF Report from the project menu to share just the PDF, without the photo files alongside it.

GPS in the Photo File (EXIF)

Every stamped photo also carries machine-readable GPS coordinates, altitude, bearing, and the capture time in its EXIF metadata. Shared photos pin correctly in Google Photos, Google Earth, GIS packages, and insurance claims software. You can turn this off in Settings if you need location-free files.

Data Export: CSV, KML, GeoJSON

From any project's menu, choose Export CSV, Export KML, or Export GeoJSON to generate a spreadsheet-ready CSV, a KML file for Google Earth, or GeoJSON for GIS tools. Each row/placemark carries the filename, timestamp, coordinates, altitude, accuracy, bearing, address, notes, and tags. Field notes are included as rows and placemarks of their own.

Project Files: Team Handoff

Hand a whole project to a teammate as a single file. No accounts, no cloud service. Stampr for Android uses the cross-platform .stamprproj format, designed so files can also move between Android phones and iPhones.

1

Share the Project File

Open the project, tap the menu, and choose Share Project File. Stampr packs every photo (stamped and original), voice note, field note with its recording, and all metadata into one .stamprproj file you can send by Quick Share, email, Drive, or anything else. Videos aren't included; share them individually.

2

Open on the Other Device

On the receiving phone, tap the file in your Files app, Drive, or the email attachment and choose Stampr. (The always-reliable path: Library → filter menu → "Import Project…".)

3

Merge Without Duplicates

Importing merges by photo identity: photos you already have are skipped, new ones are added to the matching project. Trade files back and forth as a job progresses, and you'll never get duplicates.

iPhone ↔ Android: between Android phones, project files work today. Exchanging files with iPhones requires Stampr 2.1 or later on iOS, the update that adds the cross-platform format. Earlier iOS versions (2.0 and below) use a different archive format and cannot read Android project files, or produce files Android can read.

Stampr Pro

Stampr includes 15 free photos or videos per month. Recordings draw on the same monthly allowance as stills. Field notes are free and unlimited and never use the allowance. To unlock unlimited captures and remove the watermark, purchase Stampr Pro, a one-time payment with no subscription, ever.

What you get with Pro: Unlimited photos and videos, no "Stampr" watermark, a custom text watermark or your own logo on every photo and PDF report, and right after upgrading, Stampr offers to re-stamp your existing photos to remove the free watermark from them too.

Restoring Your Purchase

If you reinstall the app or switch devices, go to Settings and tap "Restore Purchase". Your Pro access will be restored automatically via Google Play. Make sure you're signed into the same Google account you used for the original purchase.

Quick Settings Tile & Shortcuts

Quick Settings Tile

Add the Stampr Camera tile to your Quick Settings shade for the fastest path to a stamped photo: swipe down from the top of the screen, tap the edit (pencil) button, and drag the Stampr Camera tile into your panel. One tap opens the camera ready to shoot, even from the lock screen shade.

With Field Notes on, a second tile, Stampr Note, is available the same way. One tap opens the Notes tab already recording; tap the microphone to save.

App Shortcuts

Long-press the Stampr icon on your home screen for Take a stamped photo and Open the Stampr library shortcuts, plus Record a field note when Field Notes is on. You can drag any of them onto your home screen as its own one-tap icon.

Permissions

Camera

Required to take photos and videos. If access was declined, the viewfinder shows a shortcut to enable it in Android Settings.

Location (precise)

Used to stamp GPS coordinates, altitude, bearing, and street address onto photos and videos, and the position onto field notes, only while you're using the app. You can disable location in Settings if you don't need location data.

Microphone

Needed for voice notes, field notes, and video sound. Only used while recording. Voice transcription happens on-device: your audio is never sent to any server. Without microphone access you can still type field notes.

Internet

Used for street address lookup, OpenStreetMap imagery, the one-time speech model download, and Google Play Billing. Capture, stamping, and transcription all work offline. Turning off Street Address stops the lookup entirely, and Map on Stamp is off by default, so you can run Stampr without either sending a coordinate off the device.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are my photos stored?
Photos, videos, and field notes are stored in Stampr's private storage on your device. Cloud backup (Google One) covers your settings only; nothing else is uploaded anywhere. When you move to a new phone with Android's device-to-device transfer, everything comes across. To keep copies of important work, share project files or export your data, or turn on Auto-Save to Gallery to also keep copies of your stamped photos and videos in your gallery.
Do my photos sync between devices?
Not automatically: your library lives on each device, by design (nothing is uploaded). To move work across devices, share a project file (.stamprproj) from one device and open it on the other; importing merges without duplicates.
Can I share projects with iPhone users?
The .stamprproj format is cross-platform by design, but iPhone exchange needs Stampr 2.1 or later on iOS, the update that adds the cross-platform format. Once your teammate is on that version, files trade back and forth freely. Files from iOS 2.0 and earlier use a different archive format and won't open on Android.
Can I change the stamp after taking a photo?
Yes. Open the photo in the Library and edit the notes, tags, address, or project. The stamp is re-rendered from the original unstamped photo using the options it was captured with, so quality is never lost.
Why does Stampr download a file before my first voice note?
Voice transcription runs entirely on your phone using the Whisper speech model, which is about 32 MB and downloads once. After that, transcription works completely offline and your audio never leaves the device. You can start or check the download in Settings → Voice Recognition Model.
What does AI Note Cleanup need?
A device with Android's on-device AI (Gemini Nano), such as recent Pixel and Galaxy flagships. Everything runs on-device; your notes never leave the phone. On devices without it, Stampr uses the verbatim Whisper transcription, which still works on every phone, completely offline.
Does Stampr send my location data anywhere?
Nothing is transmitted to our servers; we don't have any. Your photos, voice notes, and transcripts never leave the device. Two features do look something up while you use them: the street address lookup sends the coordinate to the platform geocoder, and map imagery is fetched from OpenStreetMap. Turn off Street Address and leave Map on Stamp off and no coordinate leaves the device at all.
What are UTM and MGRS / USNG formats?
Grid coordinate systems used by surveyors, engineers, search and rescue, and emergency services. In the US, USNG is the same grid as MGRS, and Stampr writes it in the spaced USNG style (for example 18S UJ 23480 06470). Switch the coordinate format in Settings and the stamp, field notes, detail view, and PDF reports all follow. Decimal degrees and DMS remain available.
Why is the address not showing?
Street address requires an internet connection for reverse geocoding. If you're offline, the GPS coordinates will still be stamped but the address may be unavailable. You can also add or correct the address later from the photo's detail screen, and Stampr re-stamps the photo with your correction.
Can I use Stampr offline?
Yes. GPS, capture, stamping, voice transcription, and AI cleanup all work without internet (after the one-time speech model download). The only features that need connectivity are street address lookup and the OpenStreetMap imagery in the map view, map inset, and reports.
How do I share photos with the GPS report?
Open a photo and tap the share button, or select multiple photos in the Library and tap Share. The PDF report, with your photos embedded and a map overview, is attached automatically. If you only want the photos, turn off Include Report When Sharing in Settings.
Hands-free mode stops too early (or too late) on a noisy site
Hands-free listens for about two seconds of silence after you've spoken. On very loud sites the background noise can keep it recording; tap the checkmark on the recording bar to finish immediately, or the X to discard. You can always fall back to the hold-to-record prompt mode in Settings → Voice Notes.
I can't find my video
Videos are in Stampr's Library with your photos, and in their project. Stampr stamps a video right after you stop recording and confirms with "Video saved to Library". Copies go to your gallery (Movies/Stampr) only if Auto-Save to Gallery is on in Settings. If stamping ever fails, Stampr keeps the unstamped original in the Library and tells you so.
Where is the Notes tab?
It appears when Field Notes is on. Field Notes is off by default; turn it on in Settings → Capture → Field Notes. See Field Notes.
Do field notes count toward the 15 free captures a month?
No. Field notes are free and unlimited, with or without Stampr Pro. Only photos and videos use the monthly allowance.
A note says it hasn't been turned into text yet
Transcription needs the speech model on the phone (about 32 MB, downloaded once). Until it's there, recordings are kept and the Notes tab offers the download; waiting notes are transcribed as soon as it finishes. If a transcription fails, the note keeps its recording and Stampr tries again. You can also open the note and tap Transcribe.
A field note was stamped NO GPS FIX
The phone had no fresh position when the note started, and none arrived within the next minute. The note is kept with its time and words. Before recording, check the strip above the microphone: a green dot means notes will get that position. If it says Phone location is off or No location access, tap its button to fix it. Under heavy tree cover or in a canyon a fix can take longer, so give it a moment in the open if you can.
I purchased Pro but it's not showing. What do I do?
Go to Settings and tap "Restore Purchase". Make sure you're signed into the same Google account you used for the purchase. If the issue persists, contact us at support@top7systems.net.
Is my voice recording sent to Google or any server?
No. Speech recognition and AI note cleanup are performed entirely on-device. Your audio and notes never leave your phone.
