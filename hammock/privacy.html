Hammock for iPhone and iPad: Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 14, 2026

Introduction

Top 7 Systems ("we", "our", "us") built Hammock, a downtime organiser for iPhone and iPad. It was designed to be private by default. This Privacy Policy explains how Hammock handles information. The short version: Hammock has no account, collects no personal information, shows no ads and tracks no one. Your library lives on your device.

No Data Collected or Shared by Us

Hammock does not send any data about you to us. We run no server for the app, keep no user database and include no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking software. This matches the App Privacy section on the app's App Store page: Data Not Collected.

Data That Stays on the Device

Everything you create in Hammock is stored in the app's private storage on your device:

  • Your library: the items you save (titles, creators, years, cover image links, descriptions, links), their status, progress, ratings, notes and tags.
  • Lists and categories: the lists you make and any categories you rename, recolour, hide or add.
  • Logbook: the dates you started, finished or dropped things.
  • Settings: theme, appearance, layout and whether the introduction was shown.

Hammock's share extension and widgets are part of the app and use a storage area on your device that only Hammock can reach (an app group). The share extension leaves what you save there for the app to pick up; the widgets read a short list of the things you want to get to so they can show one. None of it leaves the device. Cover images are cached on the device so they work offline.

Internet Use

Hammock only uses the internet for these things:

  • Searching a catalogue: the words you type are sent to the catalogues being searched so they can return matches: Open Library (books), Wikipedia (films), TVmaze (series), Apple's public iTunes Search catalogue (podcasts and music) and the Steam store (games). If the app has been configured with keys for them, The Movie Database (TMDB) and IGDB are searched as well. Each service receives your search text and the usual connection details such as your IP address, under its own privacy policy.
  • Saving a link: when you share a link to Hammock, the page at that link is fetched once to read its title, description and image. Hammock may then look the title up in the matching catalogue above to find a poster and missing details. The requests go to that website and that catalogue, not to us.
  • Loading covers: cover images are downloaded from wherever they are hosted, usually the catalogue or the page you saved. This includes the cover the widget shows.
  • Opening a link: tapping Open link hands the address to Safari or the app that handles it.

Requests identify the app by name and version so catalogues can see where traffic comes from. They carry no identifier for you or your device. Nothing is sent if you only add items by hand.

Apple Services

  • App Store and StoreKit: distribution of the app and its updates, and processing of the optional Hammock Plus purchase, restores and Family Sharing. Ownership is read from the App Store's records on your device; we keep no purchase database and never receive your payment details.
  • Siri, Shortcuts and Control Center: when you ask Siri to pick something in Hammock or tap the Control Center button, iOS hands the request to Hammock on your device. Siri processes your voice request under Apple's terms; Hammock only receives the "Pick for me" action.
  • Crash reports: if you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may give us anonymised crash reports and usage figures. Apple controls this, and you can turn it off in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

These are subject to Apple's Privacy Policy.

Backups and New Devices

If iCloud Backup or a computer backup is turned on, Hammock's data is included in your device backup like any app's data, and moves to a new iPhone or iPad when you restore from it. Those backups are made, encrypted and managed by Apple under your control. Hammock also lets you export your own backup file to a place you choose, such as Files or iCloud Drive; that file is yours and is not sent to us.

Sharing You Control

Hammock can render a list as a picture for you to post. The picture is created on your device and shared only when you choose to, through the iOS share sheet, to the app you pick. If you choose Save Image, iOS asks once for permission to add it to your photo library; Hammock only adds pictures and cannot read your photos. Nothing is posted or transmitted automatically, and Hammock has no social features of its own.

Purchases

Hammock is free to use. A single one-time purchase, Hammock Plus, unlocks extra themes, statistics charts, custom categories and the widgets. The purchase is processed entirely by Apple through the App Store. There is no subscription.

Permissions

Hammock asks for no access to your location, contacts, calendars, camera, microphone or photos. The only prompt you may see is the one above, if you save a list picture to your photo library. Backup files are read and written only where you choose through the iOS file picker.

Children

Hammock is a general-audience app and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included, because the app collects none.

Data Deletion

Because data is stored only on your device, you are in control of it: delete individual items in the app, or remove everything by deleting the app (touch and hold its icon, then Remove App > Delete App). The Plus purchase is tied to your Apple Account and can be restored from inside the app.

Changes to This Policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. Changes will be posted on this page with an updated revision date.
