Ascent: Geometry Support

Need Help?

We are here to help you get the most out of Ascent: Geometry. Check the common questions below, or reach out to us directly. To see what the app covers and how the climb works, visit the Ascent: Geometry page. Looking for the Algebra 1 app? Its support page is here.

Unlocking the Full Course

Ascent: Geometry is free to download and free to play through Hirli: the first three legs, which cover the tools of geometry, reasoning and proof, and parallel lines. To climb past Hirli, you unlock the Full Ascent: a single one-time purchase, made inside the app through the App Store. There is no subscription, and one unlock covers every climber profile on the device. You buy it in the app, not on this website.

1

Open the unlock screen

There are two ways in. On the mountain screen, tap the The Full Ascent card that appears once you reach Hirli. Or open Settings (the gear at the top right) and choose For grown-ups. Both lead to the same place.

2

Pass the grown-ups check

A quick "For a grown-up" arithmetic question keeps the purchase in adult hands. Answer it to continue. If it is answered wrong, the app simply asks a grown-up to help, and nothing is charged.

3

Tap Unlock and pay

The Unlock button shows the one-time price for your country. Tap it, and Apple's own purchase sheet appears. Confirm with Face ID, Touch ID, or your Apple Account password. Payment is handled entirely by Apple using the payment method on your Apple Account; we never see your card details.

4

Keep climbing

The rest of the mountain opens right away, with no restart. If a parent needs to approve first through Ask to Buy, the unlock activates automatically once they do.

Already bought it? On a new device, or after reinstalling, open the same screen and tap Bought it already? Restore purchases. while signed in with the Apple Account that made the purchase. The unlock comes back at no extra charge.

If the unlock screen says The App Store isn't answering, the app could not reach the App Store to fetch the price. Check the connection, then tap Try again.

Redeeming an Unlock Code

If you have an unlock code, such as a launch promo we shared, you can redeem it in the app to unlock the Full Ascent. A promotional code may make the unlock free.

1

Open the unlock screen

Reach it the same way as a purchase: the The Full Ascent card on the mountain screen, or Settings → For grown-ups. Pass the quick grown-ups check to continue.

2

Tap "Have a code? Redeem it here."

You will find this link below the Unlock and restore options on the unlock screen.

3

Enter your code

Apple's redemption sheet opens. Type or paste your code, and the Full Ascent unlocks as soon as it is accepted.

Codes are redeemed through your Apple Account, so make sure you are signed in to the account you want the unlock tied to. Each code can be used once.

Calling the Hut for Help

Every problem carries a radio to the Hörnli Hut. When you are stuck, you call the hut and they talk you through it, one small step at a time, so you still do the thinking. Calling is never punished: no altitude is lost and nothing is marked against you.

1

Tap the Radio button

It is the radio-waves icon at the top of every problem screen. After a miss, the banner under the problem also offers Call the hut. The hut's panel slides up over your problem.

2

Tap Call the hut

The first reply is a gentle nudge: a hint that points you back at the right part of the figure or the statement without giving anything away.

3

Ask for more if you need it

Tap Ask for more to climb the hint ladder. There are four levels in all, from that first nudge up to the full worked idea. Stop at whichever level gets you moving again; the earlier rungs never state the answer.

4

Close the radio and answer

Tap Close to put the radio down and finish the problem yourself. After any answer, right or wrong, you can also tap Why? to see the worked reasoning step by step.

Reading a Figure

Every figure in Ascent: Geometry is drawn to the measures it states, so what you see is what the question means. On a phone the figure can get small once the keypad is up, so every figure has a magnifier.

1

Tap the magnifier

It is the small magnifying-glass button in the corner of the figure. The figure opens full screen, with the question underneath it.

2

Read the marks

Tick marks show congruent segments, arcs show congruent angles, a small square marks a right angle, arrowheads on two lines mean they are parallel, and dashed edges are the hidden edges of a solid. Every labeled length and angle is true to the drawing.

3

Tap Done to answer

The figure closes and the keypad is where you left it.

Building a Proof

Two-column proofs are built by tapping, not typing. The table sits in the working card, and the statements and reasons you can use sit in the keypad's place below it, so everything stays in reach.

1

Tap a statement, then its reason

Choose a statement chip (grey) and the reason that justifies it (teal). The pair appears as the next row of the proof. A row you did not mean can be removed by tapping it in the table.

2

Order is up to you

Any order that respects what each step depends on is a valid proof. If a row cites something not yet established, the app names that row when you check, so you know exactly where the argument broke.

3

Watch for circular reasoning

Citing CPCTC before the triangles are congruent, or citing the very theorem you are proving, is caught and explained rather than just marked wrong.

Proofs get harder as you master them: at first only the reasons are missing, then the pairs need ordering, and finally you build the whole proof from banks that include distractors.

Using the Scratchpad

Real climbers do rough work, and Ascent: Geometry gives you room for it. The scratchpad is private scrap paper: the app never reads or grades what you put on it.

1

Tap the Scratchpad button

It is the pencil icon at the top of every problem screen, Route Tests included. The scratchpad opens as a sheet over your problem.

2

Choose Draw or Type

Draw is for working by hand with a finger or Apple Pencil, with a pen, a highlighter, and an eraser to choose from. Type is for quick rough notes on the keyboard. Switch between them any time with the picker at the top.

3

Work it out, then come back

Sketch the figure again, write the equation, try a value, whatever helps. Tap Clear to wipe the page and start over, and Done to return to the problem and enter your answer.

The scratchpad keeps your drawing until the next problem, then wipes clean like real scrap paper. Nothing you write there is stored or seen by anyone.

Common Questions

Who is Ascent: Geometry for?

Anyone taking or retaking high school Geometry, roughly ages 13 and up. Sixty-seven skills across twelve legs cover a full US Geometry course, from angle pairs and first proofs to circles, coordinates, area, and volume. Reaching the summit means finishing the course. For Algebra 1 we make Ascent: Algebra 1, and for ages 8 to 11 Numina, our arithmetic game.

I already know some geometry. Do I have to start at the bottom?

No. When a climber starts, the app offers Find my starting point: a short, optional placement check of up to six questions that can move you up the approach, as high as the Hörnli Hut, with everything below credited in full. The ridge above the hut is always climbed. If you are new to geometry, tap Start from the beginning and begin in Zermatt. The check ends kindly at the first miss, so it never becomes a test you can fail.

What does it cost?

Ascent: Geometry is free through Hirli: the tools of geometry, reasoning and proof, and parallel lines. A single one-time purchase, the Full Ascent, opens the rest of the mountain from the Hörnli Hut to the summit. There is no subscription, no ads, and nothing else to buy.

How do I restore my purchase on a new device?

The Full Ascent is tied to your Apple Account. On a new device, open the unlock screen and tap Bought it already? Restore purchases., and make sure you are signed in with the same Apple Account that bought it. The unlock also works for every climber profile on the device.

Does the purchase work with Family Sharing and Ask to Buy?

Yes. The Full Ascent supports Family Sharing, so one purchase can cover your family group. Ask to Buy is supported too: a teen can request the unlock, and it activates automatically once a parent approves.

I'm stuck on a problem. What do I do?

Call the hut. The radio button is on every problem and is never punished: it gives four levels of help, from a small nudge to the full worked idea. Every skill also has a lesson card you can reread from the Field Guide, with a worked example, and the scratchpad gives you space for rough work with your finger or Apple Pencil.

Do I need a calculator for the trigonometry?

No. When a problem needs the value of a sine, cosine, or tangent, the problem prints the values for that angle. Choosing the right ratio and setting up the equation is the skill being practiced, so that part stays yours. Answers with roots and π are typed exactly, such as 11√3 or 7π, and any correctly simplified form counts.

How do I type an answer like a coordinate or a square root?

The keypad changes with the question. Ordered pairs get a comma key, so (7, 3) is typed as it reads. Roots and π have their own keys when a question can take them, and degree signs are added for you when the question asks for an angle. The answer line names what it wants, such as "x =" or "m∠P =", so you only type the value.

What are Route Tests and the Final Test?

A test for every leg of the route, plus the Final Test for the whole mountain, open from day one under the checklist button on the mountain screen. There is no radio, one try per problem, and nothing on the mountain moves: a test only tells you where you stand. Tests for the paid legs open with the Full Ascent.

Why am I being asked to review old skills?

That is the acclimatization system doing its job. The app schedules spaced reviews of earlier skills and mixes them into climbs, because coming back to something right before you would forget it is what makes it stick. Altitude means a skill is truly owned, not just seen once.

What is the Weather Window?

A short daily challenge of five problems, separate from the main climb, with its own streak. It is the same for every climber at your point on the route on a given day, so you can compare results. It appears after your first climb, and missing a day never takes away altitude or patches.

Can more than one person use the app?

Yes. Ascent: Geometry supports multiple climbers on one device, each with their own climb: separate progress, altitude, streaks, and patches. Switch climbers from the profile button on the mountain screen. Profiles are not accounts and need no sign-up, and one Full Ascent unlock covers all of them.

Does it work offline? Does it sync between iPhone and iPad?

It works entirely offline. An internet connection is only used for the App Store purchase itself. This version does not sync progress between devices; each device keeps its own climb.

Can I turn the sounds off?

Yes. Settings has a Sounds switch next to Haptics. The sounds also respect Silent Mode and mix with any music you have playing.

The app asked about my age. Why?

Some US states require every app to check the device's declared age range. Nothing in the app is gated by age, the answer is never stored or sent anywhere, and the system asks about once a year. See the Privacy Policy for details.

Where is my summit certificate?

After you summit, the certificate is yours to revisit any time from Settings, and you can share it with the standard iOS share sheet. Your expedition patches live in the patch gallery, also in Settings, and the Expedition Log there lists every skill on the route and where you stand with it.

The app crashed or is not responding

Make sure you are running the latest version of the app and of iOS or iPadOS. Try force-closing the app and reopening it, and if needed restart the device. A climb interrupted mid-problem is saved and banked the next time you open the app. If the problem continues, contact us with a short description of what you were doing when it happened.
