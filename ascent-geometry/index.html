Geometry around the world

In the US, Geometry is a one-year high school course. Elsewhere the same skills are spread through the geometry strand of a broader maths course. If you study under a different syllabus, here is where the climb fits.

🇺🇸

United States

This is the course itself: a complete high school Geometry aligned to the Common Core standards, as taught in grades 9 and 10, and checked against the Florida B.E.S.T., New York Regents, Virginia, Tennessee, and Louisiana blueprints.

🇬🇧

United Kingdom

The geometry and measures strand of KS3 and GCSE Maths (ages 12 to 16): angle facts, congruence and similarity, transformations, Pythagoras and trigonometry, area and volume, and the Higher-tier circle theorems.

🇦🇺

Australia

The Measurement and Space strand of Years 8 to 10 in the Australian Curriculum: congruence and similarity, Pythagoras and trigonometry, surface area and volume, and the Year 10 circle geometry.

🇪🇺

Europe

Lower secondary geometry across most of Europe, roughly ages 12 to 16: Germany's Sekundarstufe I (Kongruenz, Strahlensätze, Pythagoras, Trigonometrie), France's cycle 4 (Thalès, Pythagore, trigonométrie, transformations), and their neighbours.

🇨🇦

Canada

Grade 9 and 10 geometry and measurement in most provinces, including the trigonometry and analytic geometry of grade 10.

🇳🇿

New Zealand

The geometry and measurement of Years 9 to 11, up to the NCEA Level 1 geometric reasoning standards.

🇮🇳

India

The geometry of CBSE and ICSE classes 9 and 10: lines and angles, triangles and congruence, quadrilaterals, circles, similar triangles, coordinate geometry, an introduction to trigonometry, and surface areas and volumes.

🇿🇦

South Africa

The Euclidean geometry, similarity, Pythagoras, and measurement of CAPS grades 8 to 10, and the circle geometry that opens grade 11.

🌍

International schools

The geometry core of IGCSE Maths and the IB Middle Years Programme.

Syllabuses differ in order and emphasis, so Ascent teaches one skill at a time rather than one grade at a time. The optional placement check starts you wherever your class already is. Two-column proofs are an American habit, but the reasoning they train is the same everywhere.

One problem from every leg

Every unit of the course, photographed in play: one real question from each of the twelve legs, answered correct, at that leg's hour of the day.

The engine behind every problem

Ascent: Geometry does not use generative AI and does not ship a fixed bank of worksheet questions. Every figure, problem, hint, and worked solution comes from our own geometry engine, built for this app and checked to be exactly right.

🎲

Endless and fresh

Each of the 67 skills has its own generator that composes new problems on the spot. Practice a skill twice and you meet a different figure, while the difficulty stays true to where you are on the mountain.

📏

Drawn true, then labeled

The engine picks the real geometry first and writes the labels to match it, so a figure can never lie. Lengths, angles, arcs, and grids are measured back off the drawing in our tests, and the renderer fits every label before it draws, so nothing is ever clipped.

⚖️

Exact to the last radical

The engine computes exactly: 11√3 stays 11√3, a sector's area stays in terms of π, and a ratio stays a fraction. Answers are graded by mathematical equivalence, so any correctly simplified form counts, and a proof is graded as a chain of reasoning rather than a fixed script.

🩺

It knows the classic slips

Wrong answers are compared against the mistakes students actually make, like adding the legs instead of their squares, or taking an inscribed angle to equal its arc. When yours matches one, Ascent names the slip and how to fix it instead of just saying "try again", and a matcher only fires when the mistake is unambiguous.

The whole course is machine-verified: in our test suite an automated learner climbs all 67 skills end to end, every answer is re-derived independently of the generator that wrote it, every proof is checked both as written and in every other valid order, and a teacher read every lesson and a sample of every skill's problems before anything shipped.

Room to work

Geometry is worked out, not guessed. Every problem comes with a scratchpad, opened with a tap: open space to redraw the figure, write the equation, and show your steps before you commit to an answer.

✏️

Draw it or type it

Sketch your working with a finger or Apple Pencil, using a pen, highlighter, and eraser. Or switch to type and jot rough notes. Whatever helps you think.

🔍

See the figure large

A magnifier on every figure opens it full screen with the question underneath, so the marks are never too small to read on a phone. Tap Done and the keypad is where you left it.

🙈

Yours alone

The app never reads or grades what you write on the scratchpad. It is thinking space, not an answer box, and like real scrap paper it wipes clean for the next problem.
